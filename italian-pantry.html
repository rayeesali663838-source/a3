The southern Italian pantry

The cupboard staples behind great pasta dinners, how to choose them, and the simple techniques that turn them into silky, satisfying sauces.

Stock up

Pantry essentials

  • Dried pasta: one long shape (spaghetti) and one short (penne or rigatoni).
  • Canned tomatoes: whole or chopped plum tomatoes, plus passata.
  • Olive oil: an everyday oil for cooking and a fruity one for finishing.
  • Briny things: olives, capers and anchovies or tuna.
  • Aromatics: garlic, dried chilli and dried oregano.
  • Hard cheese and breadcrumbs: for finishing and crunch.

Briny flavour

Olives, capers and anchovies

Olives add fruity, salty depth; buy them whole and pit them yourself for the best texture. Capers come in brine or salt; rinse salted capers well before using. Anchovies dissolve into hot oil and add savoury richness without tasting fishy.

Because these ingredients are already salty, taste your sauce before adding extra salt.

Save the water

Scoop out a mugful of starchy pasta water before draining; it’s the secret to silky sauces.

Gentle garlic

Start garlic in warm, not smoking, oil. Burnt garlic turns bitter and can spoil the dish.

Finish with oil

A small drizzle of fruity olive oil just before serving lifts the whole plate.

Technique

The art of the emulsion

Italian pasta sauces cling beautifully because of an emulsion: fat (olive oil or butter) and starchy pasta water whisked together by tossing. Add the drained pasta straight to the pan, splash in some cooking water and toss over the heat until the sauce looks creamy rather than oily.

If it tightens, add another splash of water; if it’s too loose, keep tossing over the heat for a few seconds.

Cheese

Hard cheeses and their swaps

Aged hard cheeses add savoury depth. Grate them finely so they melt into the sauce rather than clumping. Traditional versions are made with animal rennet, so vegetarians should look for rennet-free alternatives.

For a dairy-free finish, toasted breadcrumbs with garlic and lemon, known as pangrattato, add crunch and richness.

Fresh or dried?

When to choose fresh pasta

Good dried pasta is ideal for pantry sauces like puttanesca, which need a firm bite. Fresh egg pasta is softer and richer, best with butter-based or delicate sauces.

Look for dried pasta with a rough, matte surface, which helps sauce cling.
