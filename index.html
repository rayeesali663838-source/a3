The building blocks

What goes into puttanesca

Six ingredients do almost all the work. Choose good versions and the sauce practically makes itself.

Olives

Gaeta or Kalamata-style black olives add depth.

Anchovies

They melt into the oil and add savoury richness.

Garlic

Sliced thinly and cooked gently, never burnt.

Chilli

A pinch of flakes brings the warmth.

Tomatoes

Good canned tomatoes are the base of the sauce.

Herbs

Parsley or basil to finish, added off the heat.

Signature recipe

Spaghetti alla puttanesca

Serves 4 · 25 minutes · Contains fish (optional)

  1. Bring a large pot of water to the boil and salt it (about 10 g per litre).

  2. Warm 4 tbsp olive oil in a wide pan; add 3 sliced garlic cloves, 4 anchovy fillets and a pinch of chilli flakes. Stir until the anchovies melt and the garlic is pale gold.

  3. Add 400 g canned chopped tomatoes and simmer for 10 minutes.

  4. Stir in a handful of pitted black olives and 1 tbsp rinsed capers; simmer for 3 more minutes.

  5. Cook 320 g spaghetti until just al dente. Transfer it to the sauce with a splash of pasta water and toss over the heat until glossy. Finish with chopped parsley.

A taste of the south

Born in the busy streets of Naples

Puttanesca is closely linked with Naples and the Campania coast, where olives, capers and tomatoes are everyday ingredients. Its exact origin story is debated, but its spirit is clear: generous flavour from simple things, cooked quickly for people you care about.

The evening ritual

Make dinner feel like an occasion

The “glow” in our name is about the warm feeling of an unhurried dinner. Light a candle, put a bowl of olives and bread on the table while the water boils, and serve the pasta straight from the pan.

Pasta waits for no one, so call everyone to the table just before you drain it.

Ask the kitchen
What is puttanesca sauce?

Puttanesca is a southern Italian pasta sauce, closely associated with Naples, made from tomatoes, olives, capers, garlic and chilli, often with anchovies. It is bold, salty and quick to make from pantry ingredients.

Can I make puttanesca without anchovies?

Yes. Leave them out for a vegetarian or vegan version, and add a few extra capers or a splash of the olive brine for a similar savoury depth.

How much salt should I add to pasta water?

A common guide is about 10 grams of salt per litre of water. Remember that sauces with olives, capers and anchovies are already salty, so taste before seasoning the sauce.

Why should I save pasta water?

The starchy cooking water helps the sauce cling to the pasta and turn glossy. Add a few spoonfuls while tossing pasta and sauce together over the heat.

Which pasta shape works best with puttanesca?

Long pasta such as spaghetti or linguine is traditional, but short shapes like penne or rigatoni hold the chunky olives and capers well too.
